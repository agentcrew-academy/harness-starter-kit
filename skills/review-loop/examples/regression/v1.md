# Community Tool Library Launch Spec v1

<!-- @block changelog -->
## What changed this version
First version, nothing to compare against. The following nine sections are the consolidated conclusions from the planning committee's three meetings.

<!-- @block first-principles -->
## Run a deletion test first
Before building anything, ask whether each piece should exist at all. Drop the deposit requirement -- what happens? Loss rates on equipment might rise, but charging a deposit turns away exactly the people who need this service most, which contradicts why it exists, so it goes. Drop the membership card -- what happens? Nobody remembers to bring it, it's dead weight, it goes. Drop the online booking system -- what happens? Things would break; popular equipment would be impossible to get on weekends, so it stays.

<!-- @block shape -->
## The service's skeleton: borrow and return
The whole service is only two actions: lending out and returning. Every rule hangs off those two actions -- don't invent a third flow. Equipment repair is part of the return process, not its own separate step.

<!-- @block inventory -->
## Equipment list
The first batch is forty items, mostly the kind of thing you use twice a year and hate storing -- drills, sanders, ladders, hand trucks. Deliberately excluding sewing machines and baking equipment; the cleanliness standards for those two categories are too contentious.

<!-- @block process -->
## Borrow-and-return flow
Book online, verify name and phone on arrival, hand over the item in person with a photo as a record. Same on return -- in person, photographed. The two photos are the only basis for resolving disputes.

<!-- @block staffing -->
## Staffing
Two regular volunteers cover weekdays, three cover weekends. Volunteers don't judge whether equipment is still usable -- they just photograph and log it. That judgment call belongs to the monthly maintenance day.

<!-- @block funding -->
## Budget
The first batch of equipment is funded by a NT$60,000 grant from the community development association. Day-to-day consumables and wear are covered by voluntary donations from borrowers, with no fixed amount required.

<!-- @block rules -->
## Hard limits
No lending to non-residents. No borrowing on someone else's behalf. Damaged equipment isn't billed for, but three damage incidents in a row from the same person suspends their borrowing privileges for three months.

<!-- @block open -->
## Still open
Who holds the roll-down shutter key, what the typhoon-day procedure is, and whether to buy public liability insurance.
